### 2. The assumption ledger

**The problem from scratch.** A rehearsal pianist, a few seconds into a
marking, knows: the exercise (its name, or its slot in the class), the
meter and feel (from one declarative line, or the first bar of counted
movement), the tempo (within about six counts of in-tempo marking), the
character, and the length — from convention plus whatever the teacher
states, and he knows which parts he does *not* know (the balance) and
either asks or plays a default. Channels: the spoken declaration; the
counted-and-moved window; the class-position prior; his memory of this
teacher. He commits **before** the piano plays and then adjusts live on
the dancers. What the recording never contains: the tacit length of the
unstated back side (barre-6 F6).

### 3. Ideas — each with the five fields

Lens tags: **[room]** the musician in the room · **[field]** another
field's solved problem · **[eval]** the evaluation · **[data]** the data ·
**[product]** · **[inverse]**.

**I1 [data] [field: MIR] Let the piano label itself — and be the answer
key.** *Observation:* §1.4, §1.7, assumption L; 17 takes carry
`played_bpm` labelled by ear. *Mechanism:* run the W3 benchmark trackers
(Essentia RhythmExtractor2013, madmom `min_bpm=40`, librosa PLP, Beat
This!) on the take audio directly and on a Demucs `--two-stems vocals`
non-vocal stem (the 6-source piano stem is documented as poor — outward
scan), scored against `played_bpm` with Acc1/Acc2 and the piano beats
kept as a candidate grid the owner *corrects* instead of taps. *Kill
test:* 17 barre-6 takes on the owner's machine (media), an extension of
`scripts/baseline_benchmark.py`, ~1 h machine, **0 owner hours**.
*Killing result:* best tool Acc2@8% < 0.6 on the takes (the pipeline is
6/17). *Cost of being wrong:* an afternoon. If it works, every future
accompanied class labels its own tempo — the corpus's binding constraint.

**I2 [eval] Score the demo on its in-tempo window, not the whole clip.**
*Observation:* §1.3, §1.7 (F4). *Mechanism:* the 9 demo cases carry
owner-annotated intended-tempo spans in `notes`; replay each frozen demo
trace restricted to that span (W13(b)'s prefix machinery truncates by
word end; a span filter is a small extension) and compare with the
whole-clip answer. *Kill test:* 9 demos, offline, frozen traces, no
media, **0 owner hours**, ~1 h. *Killing result:* tempo right on ≤ 4 of 9
with the window handed over (3/9 now) → the loss is estimation, not
segmentation, and R1 is not where it lives. *Non-killing result:*
segmentation is the loss — the capture-penalty diagnostic has been
running inside every clip. *Cost of being wrong:* an hour.

**I3 [room] Commit at six counts.** *Observation:* §1.9; F5; Drake &
Botte. *Mechanism:* the decision the pianist makes is "what do I know
six counted beats into the in-tempo span"; score tempo, division and
grouping at exactly that prefix, against the label, as the headline for
demos. It replaces "when to stop" (W14, negative) with "score at the
moment the pianist must play". *Kill test:* same replay machinery as I2,
9 demos + 26 rig, **0 owner hours**. *Killing result:* six-count
accuracy on rig below half the whole-clip accuracy → the evidence is not
there that early for any estimator, and the stopping-rule line dies with
it. *Cost of being wrong:* an hour.

**I4 [eval] [product] Playability, not fields.** *Observation:* §1.3,
§1.7, owner rulings (2/4 ≡ 4/4; the marking is the spec). *Mechanism:*
one composite per case — tempo within 8% at a rung the owner accepts as
playable *for that exercise* (a per-exercise ruling), division right,
phrase length within one phrase — and report the between-levels mass
(1.1–1.8×) as its own bin, because that is the unplayable error. *Kill
test:* rescoring the blessed run offline; **20 owner minutes** of
per-exercise "which rungs are playable" rulings. *Killing result:* the
composite orders the 52 rows the same as committed tempo accuracy. *Cost
of being wrong:* an hour plus twenty owner minutes.

**I5 [inverse] If the joint posterior cannot hear the piano, ask what
does.** *Observation:* §1.4, §1.8; W3-remainder (nuclei_hybrid F 0.727
raw, Essentia Acc1 0.82 on rig). *Mechanism:* run the seven-tool
benchmark on all 26 barre-6 clips against case labels, demos and takes
reported separately. *Kill test:* owner's machine, ~1 h, **0 owner
hours**. *Killing result:* no shelf tool beats 9/26 on demos → the
material is the wall, not the method. *Non-killing on takes:* merges
into I1. *Cost of being wrong:* an hour.

**I6 [field: statistics] Set the ask-band by conformal abstention, not
by 0.55/0.80.** *Observation:* §1.2. *Mechanism:* split-conformal
threshold on the posterior-mass score over the ~50 verified rows gives
"commit only when the accepted set contains the truth 90% of the time"
with an honest small-n guarantee (outward scan: conformal abstention /
selective conformal risk control, 2025). *Kill test:* one offline script,
**0 owner hours**. *Killing result:* at 90% the rule abstains on > 70% of
barre-6 rows → confidence carries too little information to gate
anything and the ≤ 4 questions/class budget is unreachable. *Cost:* an
hour.

**I7 [data] Restore an anchor-free cohort.** *Observation:* §1.5.
*Mechanism:* owner queue item 5 (tap three or four barre-6 grids) with
one added ruling — **from scratch, not by correcting the seeds** — so the
extractor finally has non-circular truth on a second voice over piano;
then `stage1-peakrate` on the owner's machine. *Kill test:* 4 grids,
**~1 owner hour**. *Killing result:* R@tac < 0.5 on a real teacher. *Cost
of being wrong:* one owner hour, which item 5 costs anyway.

### 6. Questions for the owner — the pool

Ranked by how much the answer changes what to ask next. Private notes
on what each answer would change.

1. **(opening)** In the clips where the system read a tempo about one and
   a half times what you wrote down, what was going on in the room — a
   teacher marking cleanly in tempo and the machine mis-hearing, or a
   marking that was drifting, talking, or counted at a different level
   from what got played? → *clean marking* ⇒ I2/I3/I1 (estimation vs
   segmentation); *off-tempo marking* ⇒ the marking gap is the product
   problem and per-teacher memory outranks perception.
2. In the takes, is the teacher counting *with* the piano in tempo, or
   talking free over it? → whether take transcripts are beat evidence at
   all, or noise to subtract (I1).
3. How many counts of in-tempo marking does a teacher owe you before you
   can play, and what do you do when you do not get them? → I3's fixed
   moment; product ask-vs-default (F4: 3.6 s on the plié).
4. When the demo does not state the length (balance, back side), what do
   you play? → whether `counts` should gate on demo rows at all (I4).
5. Is a wrong rung ever playable — and is 1.4× ever playable? → I4's
   playable set; he defines it.
6. Did the Barre-6 pianist follow the marking or his own sense of the
   exercise — would you have played 79 for the frappé marked at 135? →
   whether the take is a usable tempo answer key (I1).
7. Would you rather the system be right on one demo in three and silent
   otherwise, or ask one word on two in three? → the abstention band
   (I6); product posture.
8. What is the one word you would want to be asked? → the question
   grammar; which field needs confidence most.
9. One more class to capture: this teacher again (calibration) or a new
   one (generalization)? → corpus organization; Vision 05 §5.6.
10. When you tap the barre-6 grids, from scratch or by correcting the
    machine's marks? → I7; he needs the anchoring evidence to choose.
