### 2. The assumption ledger

**The problem from scratch.** A rehearsal pianist a few seconds into a
marking knows the exercise (its name or its slot), the meter and feel
(one declarative line, or the first counted bar), the tempo (within about
six counts of in-tempo marking), the character and the length — from
convention plus whatever the teacher states — and knows which parts he
does *not* know (the balance) and asks or plays a default. He commits
**before** the piano plays. What he uses to tell a beat syllable from the
syllables between beats is **not recorded anywhere in this repository**:
every trace so far has asked *when* he knew, not *by what cue*.

### 3. Ideas — each with the five fields

Lens tags: **[room]** the musician in the room · **[field]** another
field's solved problem · **[eval]** the evaluation · **[data]** ·
**[product]** · **[inverse]**.

**I1 [eval] [field: MIR — Zapata et al. 2012; Holzapfel et al. 2012] The
per-clip forensic matrix as a standing instrument, with method
*disagreement* as the signal.** *Observation:* §1.1–§1.3. *Mechanism:*
the 22-method × 34-row table is already on disk in four JSONs; assembled
once per increment it shows, per clip, which readings exist and where the
channels split. Zapata's MaxMA selects the committee member that agrees
most with the rest; Holzapfel's selective sampling spends annotation where
the committee *disagrees*. Here the disagreeing clips are exactly the
three the owner should listen to, and the agreeing-but-wrong ones are the
ones a vote can never fix. The owner's own framing — "fine-tune which
algorithm works on which clip" — is fitting at n = 8; the honest form is
**regime → method**, with the regime read from a signal (events per beat,
proximity to the band edge, within-clip tempo movement), and the matrix is
the map the regimes are read from. *Cheapest kill test:* extend the
assembly to all 34 rows and score whether disagreement (spread of the
readings in log-tempo) separates the shipping path's right rows from its
wrong ones; ~1 h, **0 owner hours**. *Killing result:* AUC < 0.6 — then
the matrix is a picture, not an instrument. *Cost of being wrong:* an hour.

**I2 [eval] Band-edge slack, pre-registered** (AP-1 §8.1, still unrun).
*Observation:* §1.5. *Mechanism:* refuse factor 1 only above 140 ×
(1 + slack); frappé flips for three estimators. *Kill test:* rescore the
EB-1 and AP-1 JSONs offline at slack ∈ {5 %, 8 %}; 10 min, **0 owner
hours**. *Killing result:* any currently-right rig row goes wrong (a raw
in 141–151 that should have halved). *Cost:* minutes. This is Standing
Lesson 2 applied to the diagnostic harnesses; the shipping path already
carries W9's soft prior.

**I3 [room] [inverse] Two channels, one tie-breaker: fuse the word path
and the acoustic path, and break ties with the exercise prior — never
with a vote.** *Observation:* §1.2, §1.3, §1.10. *Mechanism:* keep the
shipping (word/marker) estimate and an all-pairs or comb acoustic estimate
as two candidates. When they agree within 8 %, commit. When they disagree
— plié (125 vs ≈94), frappé, coupé-barre, tendu, fondu, rond-de-jambe —
the tie is broken by the exercise-conditioned prior of pulse-next-step
§6/§7, keyed off Gemini's exercise label (6 of 8 right). *Kill test:*
offline over the frozen matrix once the owner's blind table exists; until
then the *oracle* tie-breaker (true exercise, a wide range in step-one
units) bounds the ceiling — does agree-or-prior reach 7–8 of 8 on demos
while holding 24 of 26 on rig? ~1 h, **0 owner hours beyond the table**.
*Killing result:* the prior ranges contain both candidates on the
disagreeing clips, so the prior cannot break the tie → a third channel is
needed. *Cost of being wrong:* an hour. *Caveat:* n = 8, and §1.7's units
trap.

**I4 [field: speech prosody] Measure whether the on-beat syllable is
acoustically marked before building anything that weights it.**
*Observation:* §1.8 (865 extra vs 235 on-beat), assumption J; Lidji et al.
2011 (Review 1 §2.9: tappers entrain to speech at the *stressed-syllable*
level). *Mechanism:* the `machine-hearing` labels already tag every
peakRate event as on-beat or extra; with the media on this machine,
measure intensity, F0 and duration per event and the separability (AUC)
of on-beat from extra. The off-the-shelf version is the wavelet prosody
toolkit (MIT; needs word alignments, which the traces carry — outward
scan). *Kill test:* one script, 8 demos, ~1 h, **0 owner hours**.
*Killing result:* AUC < 0.65 — her stress does not mark the beat; the
acoustic channel cannot separate beats from syllables on its own and I3's
tie-breaker must come from outside. *Non-killing:* AUC > 0.75 — a
stress-*weighted* all-pairs is the next pre-registered increment. *Cost:*
an hour. *Adjacent to a held line, flagged:* the 2026-09-02 amendment
holds "accent-periodicity / prominence work" until step two; that line is
about *bar* accent for meter. This is beat-vs-syllable for the pulse. The
owner rules whether it is inside the hold.

**I5 [room] The count-in as the machine's window anchor.** *Observation:*
SW-1 (audio-regularity windows are not his spans; his spans read with his
taps are good); W15 (the count-in "seven and eight" opens 6 clips and is
un-parsed); W13(a) (a declarative line, then counted movement).
*Mechanism:* start the window at the first counted number or count-in
token in the frozen transcript and read the next N seconds with
all-pairs; compare the anchor to the owner's span starts. *Kill test:*
transcripts + case-note spans, ~30 min, **0 owner hours**. *Killing
result:* the first-count token lands more than 3 s from his span start on
most demos. *Cost:* 30 minutes. Not a window sweep: SW-1 varied the
*length* under an audio-regularity pick; this varies the *anchor*.

**I6 [inverse] Sparse voicing is a latent-grid problem: run the
ADR-017 lattice on the acoustic events for the three voiced-1-and-3
demos.** *Observation:* AP-1 and EB-1 (period fitting fails plié; comb
passes rond-de-jambe only via the bar ×3); Review 6 §6 ("already built
here and not used for tempo"). *Mechanism:* feed peakRate events as the
evidence class into `posterior.py` and read the MAP tempo; the W11
sidecar path exists for rig, and the `machine-hearing` files carry the
demo events. *Kill test:* offline, 8 demos, ~2 h, **0 owner hours**.
*Killing result:* the lattice locks to the syllable level on the clutter
clips and still fails plié → the sparse mode needs the prior, not the
lattice. *Cost:* two hours. ADR-017 names the acoustic evidence class as
"the path this ADR did not deliver"; this is that path, not a re-run.

**I7 [data] [product] Rule the drift convention.** *Observation:* §1.9.
*Mechanism:* a one-sentence owner ruling — start, settle, or the moment
of commitment — and two labels follow it. *Kill test:* none; ~5 owner
minutes. *Cost of being wrong:* two labels. (Carried as pool question 2.)

**I8 [eval] Fix the stage1 headline line.** *Observation:* §1.6.
EVAL-CHANGE, trivial; report the verified aggregate first. *Cost:* none.

**Finds (label · what it claims · where it lands):**

- Zapata, Holzapfel, Davies, Oliveira & Gouyon, "Assigning a Confidence
  Threshold on Automatic Beat Annotation in Large Datasets", ISMIR 2012 —
  **read** (PDF). A committee of five trackers; mutual agreement measured
  by pairwise Information Gain; the member with maximum agreement (MaxMA)
  beats the best single tracker by ≈3.3 % AMLt below MMA 2.4 bits; an MMA
  of 1.5 bits separates perceptually acceptable output (listening test,
  25 participants). → I1; and §1.2 shows why the *selection* half does
  not transfer to the hard demos here (correlated errors), while the
  *disagreement-as-difficulty* half (their earlier "selective sampling",
  already in Review 2 §4.4) does.
  http://mtg.upf.edu/system/files/publications/Jose_Zapata_et_al_157_ISMIR_2012.pdf
- Suni, Šimko, Aalto & Vainio, wavelet prosody toolkit (Computer Speech &
  Language 2017; GitHub `asuni/wavelet_prosody_toolkit`) — **read**
  (README). MIT licence; input WAV + word/syllable alignments (HTK lab or
  TextGrid); output continuous prominence and boundary strength per unit;
  Python 3, pycwt/soundfile/tgt; last update 2020. Installs in the repo
  environment in principle (untested). → I4's off-the-shelf arm; Whisper
  words supply the alignment.
  https://github.com/asuni/wavelet_prosody_toolkit
- Bolt, Pauwels & Fazekas, "Beat and Downbeat Detection: A Reformulated
  Approach", ICASSP 2026 — **abstract-only**. Neural trackers emit invalid
  mixtures of competing beat grids; a masked-diffusion formulation models
  multiple plausible grids and builds one coherent answer by iterative
  inference. → confirms Review 6 §4's "competing hypotheses with
  switching" and the posterior line over point estimators; no tool to run.
  https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11461608/
- "A Contrastive Self-Supervised Learning scheme for beat tracking
  amenable to few-shot learning", ISMIR 2024 (arXiv 2411.04152) —
  **abstract-only**. Contrastive pre-training on unlabelled music, then
  fine-tuning with a few annotated examples. → parked: the pre-training is
  music, the adaptation set here would be eight demos.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04152
- "Music Tempo Estimation on Solo Instrumental Performance" (arXiv
  2504.18502, WASPAA-rejected, posted 2025) — **abstract-only**. Models
